# Implementation status

What the environment implements against the [rules reference](README.md), and where it
knowingly departs.

Status values:

- **implemented** — the environment follows the rule.
- **partial** — some of the rule is in place; the missing part is named.
- **divergent** — the environment does something *different* on purpose. Not a bug.
- **absent** — nothing in the environment corresponds to the rule.

Every **divergent** row is a deliberate simplification, and saying so is the point: this
table is the roadmap for the next implementation phase.

| [Cannot cross the board edge](03-moving.md#making-a-move) | implemented | Positions are clipped to the board. |
| [Cannot move through enemy models](03-moving.md#making-a-move) | implemented | `domain/movement.py:resolve_move` treats enemy bases as **blockers**: the move stops at base contact. Friendly bases are `passable` — crossable but not endable-on — **which is what the rules say** (03-moving.md § Making a move: "it may pass through friendly models"), and which also avoids a squad gridlocking on its own front rank. Verified empirically: a model at (20,20) asked to move +6" at an enemy centred 3" away ends 1.74" along, exactly `3 - 0.63 - 0.63`. Gated on `base_radius > 0`, which defaults to the rules' 32mm infantry base, so this is on in every config. ⚠ This row read *absent — movement ignores occupancy entirely* until 2026-08-22; it predated bases. ⚠ The first correction of it claimed the rules do not permit passing through friendly models. They do, in the same section — that was my error, not the spec's. |
| [Coherency (2" / 9")](03-moving.md#coherency) | **partial** | The predicate is exact — `domain/coherency.py` evaluates both conditions *and* the single-connected-group clause, base to base, over alive models only, at the `coherency` config block's distances (defaulting to the rules' 2"/9"). `just measure-coherency` reports it for any policy. What is enforced is **deployment only**, via `coherency.enforce_at_deployment` (§ Setting up), and it is **off by default** — turning it on is a scenario change that moves every baseline. The **end-of-move revert** (§ Making a move) is implemented behind `coherency.enforce_move`, in three modes: `revert_unit` (the spec — one model out of place cancels the unit's move), `revert_model` (a divergence returning the models in breach) and `repair` (the largest divergence — it pulls stray models onto the unit body and reverts only when the unit cannot be gathered, so unlike either revert it can *restore* coherency rather than only refuse to break it; on nine held-out tables it takes a trained agent −34.8 → −4.5 vp_margin, but it moves every policy on the board, so it is a scenario change). A third mode, `clamp`, *shortened* the move instead of cancelling it; it was removed on 2026-08-16 once all three measured the same price and enforcement was established as a play-time referee rather than a training lever, which made the choice of mode a detail. **`revert_unit`, the spec's own mode, is free relative to the divergence** — on nine held-out tables with four checkpoints (two per warm-start lineage) the referee costs ~7 vp either way: off 87.2, `revert_model` 80.4, `revert_unit` **79.4**, 1 vp apart with the sign flipping across checkpoints. So use the spec mode at play; the divergence buys nothing. **The price of the rule is measured and was the same for all three**: on the real-tables scenario with a checkpoint that never saw the constraint, `vp_margin` +104.2 → +76.7 / +75.8 / +78.3 respectively, so the cost is not the revert's abruptness. **⚠ Enforce at play, never during training.** Ten runs on the real tables: measured with the referee *off*, so the numbers describe the policy rather than the wrapper, a policy trained under enforcement intends **0.569** units coherent against **0.756–0.886** for `objective_hold.require_coherent` alone, and loses on unseen ground too — **70.3** vp_margin on nine held-out tables against gate-only's **81.5**. Every reverted action produces the identical outcome, so they share an advantage and the policy gradient inside that set is exactly zero; only the entropy bonus acts there. Enforcement guarantees a legal board and teaches nothing. It is not the learned policy's either — the *scripted* bar pays the same toll (+92.2 → +67.5 under `clamp`), which makes ~25 vp the price of coherency on that mission rather than evidence of a policy trapped by it. Score any enforced arm against a bar measured **on the enforced config**. Selection **iterates to a fixed point** in both modes — reverting a model changes whether its unit is coherent, so `revert_model` re-checks and escalates to the whole unit when a local revert leaves the unit broken; a single pass enforced nothing, breaking units at the same rate as no enforcement at all. The guarantee is *this move did not break the unit*, not *the unit is coherent*: a unit already split by casualties cannot be repaired by a revert, which is what attrition is for. **§ Regaining coherency's attrition** is implemented behind `coherency.attrition`, at the last phase boundary of a player's turn (there is no End of Turn phase on this clock, and adding one would change episode length). Removal is deterministic — smallest chain component first, then the model furthest from the rest — and the casualties are **not** credited to the opponent, per the rule that they trigger nothing firing on destruction. Remaining divergences: the check happens at a phase boundary rather than a real End of Turn step, and a failed *deployment* raises rather than removing the unit from the board. `group_max_distance` + `group_cohesion` remain as the old one-distance approximation, and price only the nearest neighbour. **The cheapest way to play this rule legally is not enforcement at all but a legal decode**: `model/common/decoding.py` picks the most probable *legal* combination of per-model moves (top-3 each, 243 combinations per five-model unit), which is worth **+26.8 vp_margin and coherency 0.651 → 0.847** on three seeds over nine held-out tables, for no weight change and no divergence from the rule. |
| [Set up in coherency](03-moving.md#setting-up) | **partial** | `coherency.enforce_at_deployment` places every unit in coherency by construction — each model lands within the chain distance of an already-placed squadmate and within the spread cap of all of them. Default placement does not: measured at **0 of 20 episodes** coherent on the golden shooting config, which is why even a policy that never moves scored 0.006. With it on, the same policy scores 0.992. On failure this raises rather than removing the unit from the board, as the rules would. |
| [Engagement range 2" / 5"](03-moving.md#engagement) | **divergent** | `engagement_range` in `envs/types/config/env.py`, authored in inches and resolved through the scale. Defaults to **1**, not the rules' 2, because every baseline and trained result in the repo was measured at 1 — adopting the rules value changes which shots are legal and is a scenario change to be measured, not a correction. No vertical component. Used only to gate shooting. |
| [Remain stationary](09-movement-phase.md#remain-stationary) | implemented | The `"stay"` action slice, `STAY_ACTION = 0`. |
| [Normal move](09-movement-phase.md#normal-move) | implemented | The `"movement"` action slice. |
| [Advance move](09-movement-phase.md#advance-move) | **partial** | `n_advance_speed_bins` (default **0**, opt-in per scenario) registers an `advance` action slice: `n_movement_angles x n_advance_speed_bins`, valid in the movement phase. Distance is an **absolute rung above the model's Move**: `M + (bin + 1) x (6 / bins)`, so at `M = 6` with three bins the ladder is 8"/10"/12". One D6 is rolled per UNIT at the command→movement boundary and gates which rungs are **legal** (`ActionHandler.advance_legality`, masked on both seats), rather than deciding what an action means. ⚠ **Re-encoded 2026-08-23**, replacing `fraction x (M + roll)`, which had two defects: it admitted **dominated** actions (a 4" advance costs the turn's shooting and buys nothing a 4" normal move would not — half the slice in expectation) and it was the only slice in the game whose action indices changed meaning turn to turn, so a policy had to read `advance_roll` to know what its own action did. The reason recorded for admitting dominated bins — that a unit which cannot stop short cannot advance and halt to keep coherency — was checked against `env.step` and does **not** hold: only one model need choose an advance for the unit to advance, and its squadmates keep the whole normal slice. ⚠ At three bins a roll of 1 leaves **no** legal rung; the rules would permit a 7" advance and the ladder cannot express it, which is a resolution limit and not a bug — a 1" gain never repays a turn of fire. ⚠ The re-encoding is a **no-op for any non-advancing policy** (`squad_march_take` scores −2.8 / `held` 2.57 / `alive` 0.396 / `coherent` 0.845 on all 45 tables either side of it) but **voids the advance arm's checkpoints behaviourally** — the tensor width is unchanged so they still load, and their action indices now mean different distances. Advancing sets `advanced_this_turn`, which both shooting masks already read, so it forfeits the turn's shooting — correct here because no weapon has the ability that would permit firing after an advance. ⚠ The slice is appended **after** shooting so no existing action index moves; widening `n_speed_bins` instead would renumber everything (`decode_action` is angle-major, speed-minor) and warm starts load with `strict=False`. ⚠ The dice come from a **dedicated RNG stream**, drawn only when bins > 0, so no existing config's terrain, deployment or combat rolls shift. The roll and the spent-shooting flag are **both in the observation** when bins > 0 (`advance_roll`, normalised by the die so it is not 6x the scale of its neighbours, and `advanced_this_turn` for the value head — the action mask already forbids the shot). ⚠ **An advance scenario CANNOT be paired against a non-advance control.** Turning bins on changes the ACTION space (101 → 149 on `4v4_two_phases`), so the output head is a different shape and the first sampled action can differ; a zero-initialised conditioning path fixes an added *input* but cannot fix an added *action*. Compare unpaired, with more seeds, against a control retrained at the same epoch budget. Advance is **resolved to unit level**: if any model in a unit picks an advance bin, the whole unit advances and the whole unit forfeits its shooting, so "advance one model and keep the rest firing" is not available. ⚠ **Still divergent:** each model still chooses its own distance independently, where the rules have the unit make one move type — so a 12"-versus-6" split inside a 2" chain will break coherency. The free lunch is gone; the divergence is not. And an advancing unit should also be unable to declare a charge; the charge phase is a stub, so nothing enforces that yet — whatever implements it must read `advanced_this_turn`. |

| Rule | Status | Owner / note |

| [Line of sight](06-visibility-and-damage.md#visibility) | **divergent** | **Only terrain blocks; models never do.** The rules let enemy and third-party models block, with the observer's own unit and the target's exempt. Neither applies here: no model on this table has an opaque silhouette — a 32mm infantry figure is a thin shape on a round base rather than a cylinder, so a line that clips a base can be drawn in practice — and with nothing occluding, the *ignore your own unit* exemption has nothing to exempt. Three rays per pair give the three-state answer (hidden / cover / visible); the rays are a parallel corridor of the pair's width rather than tangents to the target, so sight stays exactly symmetric — the metrics depend on that. No height, so nothing is *fully* visible by the rules' vertical test. |
| [Terrain categories](13-terrain.md#terrain-categories) | **divergent** | One category. `Footprint` (`envs/domain/terrain.py`) is a closed outline — concave allowed — that blocks line of sight. |
| [Terrain and movement](13-terrain.md#terrain-and-movement) | absent | Movement ignores terrain completely — models pass through footprints freely. |
| [See out of and into a feature](13-terrain.md#obscuring) | **divergent** | `domain/sight.py` exempts any footprint containing either endpoint (`polygons_contain_points`, edge-inclusive). Two divergences, and the second is the load-bearing one. **The exemption belongs to obscuring, and is applied to every feature** — the spec grants see-out/see-into in the *Obscuring* section, while *Solid* has no such clause ("line of sight cannot be drawn across any enclosed gap"). With one terrain category there is nothing to withhold it from. **And it exempts the whole piece, not the model's own position**: a model standing inside a 20"-wide ruin sees straight through all 20" of it, verified — outside→outside across the same piece is correctly blocked, inside→anywhere is not. Per-piece, so a *second* ruin on the line still blocks, which bounds it. **Consequential on the real tables specifically**: every one of the 270 objective markers in `configs/evaluation/maps/` sits inside a ruin and on 24 of 45 tables the two centre markers share the largest central piece, so a unit holding the middle objective is standing *inside* that building while it fires through it. This is what "shooting through terrain" looks like in a recording. Fixing it changes LOS on every eval map and voids the baselines measured there. No height, so the 3" threshold has no analogue. **Measured before #193**, which restored the closing edge dropped from padded polygon outlines: the containment test that fires this exemption was over-reporting, so the exemption fired more often than the design alone calls for. On 8 real layouts the fix took grid points reading "inside a piece" 666 → 516 and visible LOS pairs 219,379 → **169,789 (−22.6%)**. The mechanism is unchanged and its test still passes (a single 4-vertex outline needs no padding), but the share of the recording attributable to the *design* rather than to that bug has not been re-measured. |
| [Obscuring](13-terrain.md#obscuring) | **divergent** | Achieved by the same footprint blocking, keyed off the feature rather than an enclosing terrain area. |

Since models stopped occluding it is granted by **terrain** alone, which is what the chapter describes. Requires `base_radius > 0`: with dimensionless models the three rays coincide and cover cannot occur. |
