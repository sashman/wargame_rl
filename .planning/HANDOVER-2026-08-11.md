## What is established (do not re-derive)

1. **`objective_hold` 1.25 -> 2.5 with `objective_coverage` 0.3 -> 0.1 is worth
   +9.8 vp**, both seeds, replicated across two scenarios (+5.2 on the first).
   Moving income from a *global* term to a *per-model* one sharpens the gradient.
2. **Terrain must be fitted to the 45 real layouts** in `configs/evaluation/maps/`,
   never tuned against `just measure-terrain` — that metric rewards piece count
   and produced 0.194 hidden against the real game's 0.088. See
   `reports/2026-08-10-real-terrain-and-the-abandonment-gap.md`.
3. **The agent does not use cover; it manages range.** `blocked_share` 0.738
   against the bar's 0.735 — identical. What differs is how often it is in range
   at all (0.394 v 0.679). Measured on realistic terrain, so the 2026-08-05
   finding now stands on the right environment.
4. **Objective selection must be mirror-symmetric by construction.** Maximising
   spread unconstrained put 2 objectives on one side in 38/200 layouts. Pinned by
   a test.

## What is refuted (do not retry)

- `overstack_penalty_per_extra` -> 0.0 — **null** (+49.4 v control +50.6).
- **Spreading objectives fixes abandonment** — it does not. Measured at 3.6 sigma
  as a natural experiment, it did not transfer to training.
- **Raising `progress_scale`** — monotonically *worse*: 6.0 -> 12.0 -> 17.0 gives
  46.7 -> 35.4 -> 32.1 at epoch ~270.
- `fallback_to_nearest: false` — dropped before running: unassigned models get
  `overstack_penalty` and **zero** progress reward, and group assignment gives
  each objective to one group, so 2 of 5 squads would earn only a penalty.
- **`crowding_exponent` is not the suspect.** At `a=1.0` the pot is conserved, so
  the ninth model on a point already earns a ninth of a lone model's pay.

## The invariant, and the diagnosis

**Abandonment is 0.380 / 0.370 / 0.357** across five weight configurations and
two scenarios, against the bar's **0.147**. The agent is pushed off an objective
**1.7%** of the time against the bar's **24.7%** — it is not losing fights over
objectives, it is not turning up. It parks ~9 surplus models on the ones it holds
(bar: 3.9) while winning the firefight ~2:1 and keeping a fifth more models alive.

Per-model income says why (probe, n=15 episodes):

| activity | reward/step |
|---|---|
| alone on an objective | **1.30** |
| on a crowded one (8 others) | 0.62 |
| loitering outside | 0.50 |
| **in transit** | **0.26** |

The crowding gradient *works* (1.30 alone -> 0.36 at twelve occupants), and
leaving is profitable over a 20-round episode: ~1.26 of loss over a ~3.3 round
walk, then +0.66/step, breaking even ~1.9 rounds after arrival.

**So the reward is not mis-weighted — the landscape has a trough between two
peaks and the policy sits on the smaller one.** Crossing needs a *sustained*
multi-round walk that single-step exploration is punished for starting.

## The next step, agreed but NOT started

**Pin a model's target objective once assigned**, in
`closest_objective_v2._choose_target_objective`.

Two reasons:
- **98.4%** of models standing on an objective have that same objective as their
  assigned target (`fallback_to_nearest: true` means a model on an objective is
  always nearest to it), so the term that should pull surplus models away instead
  confirms their position and pays them progress for a journey of zero length.
- It would make the potential-based argument **actually true** — see the error
  below.

This is a code change, not a config flag. **Write the test that pins the
switching rate first.** The user asked to be consulted before it starts.

## The reasoning error worth remembering

I justified raising `progress_scale` hard because progress is potential-based and
"telescopes to `scale * (initial - final distance)`, so oscillation cannot farm
it". **That holds only if the potential is a fixed function of state, and it is
not.** `target_switched` measures **0.273/step in transit** — models re-base their
target constantly, so the shaping is farmable and raising the scale amplifies the
farm. Generalised: *a shaping term keyed on an assigned target is not
potential-based at all if the assignment can change mid-episode.*

## Gotchas that cost time today

- **`pgrep -f "train.py..."` matches the `just` wrapper shells**, so watchers
  self-match and never fire, and kill commands hit wrappers not trainers. Use
  `ps -eo comm=,args= | awk '$1 ~ /^python3?$/ && /train\.py/'`.
- **SIGINT deadlocks the trainers** in a loguru reentrancy storm — they hold GPU
  memory at 0% utilisation. Use SIGKILL.
- **`eval/vp_margin` and `eval/baseline_*_vp_margin` are not comparable** — 30
  episodes at seeds 500000+ against 20 at 10000+. Score with
  `just measure-checkpoint` against the recorded n=100 table instead.
- **`reward/components/closest_objective_v2/target_obj_idx` is a diagnostic**
  (an objective index, -1.0 when unset), not reward. It dominates any naive
  component sum.
- Do not query the Wandb API while runs train; read `wandb/run-*/files/` instead.
